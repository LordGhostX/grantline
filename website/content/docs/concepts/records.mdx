---
title: Records
description: Records connect authority, proposals, decisions, approvals, and execution outcomes over time.
---

Grantline needs a record of how authority was used, not only a decision made in isolation. Records connect the proposal, the authority behind it, the conditions considered, any approval, and the eventual execution outcome.

## Traceability

Authority changes, approvals, custody changes, and successful execution can produce durable records that others can inspect. These records make it possible to follow the path from a granted boundary to the action that used it.

Records can be stored and presented in different ways, but their purpose remains the same: showing what authority was involved and what happened after the decision.

## Failed paths

A read-only evaluation can return `DENY` without creating a state change. A submitted execution can also fail before completion, so the absence of a successful execution record does not mean no proposal was evaluated.

A record should distinguish an authorisation result from an execution outcome, because a failed attempt may not create a permanent record.

## From records to explanations

Records can also be aggregated into a human-readable explanation of a decision, connecting the proposal, authority lineage, checks, approval, transaction, and outcome.

See [decisions](/docs/concepts/decisions) for the distinction between authorisation and execution.
